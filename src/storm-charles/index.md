# Storm Charles, archived

## What happened

IPMA named the low-pressure system CHARLES in a communiqué on 9 November (13:17 UTC) and said orange warnings would follow. On 10 November IPMA issued red warnings for the Lisbon district for Wednesday 11 November, first for wind and then for rain, and the government declared an alert situation (situação de alerta) for all of mainland Portugal for that day. Web Summit said its Day 2 on Wednesday would not take place, on the government's formal advice, and on Wednesday said it would return for its final day on Thursday 12 November.

On Wednesday the storm turned out weaker than first forecast: IPMA's communiqué no. 3 (12:16 UTC) said the depression had split into two low-pressure centres, so the wind should be weaker than forecast the day before. IPMA lowered Lisbon's wind warning from red to orange early on Wednesday and later shortened and lowered the other warnings. By 18:00 UTC only the sea warning remained. At its 12:00 situation update, ANEPC counted 538 incidents, none significant, with Greater Lisbon, the Setúbal peninsula and the Algarve most affected.

### The government, ANEPC and the Maritime Authority

- **The government** declared an alert situation for all of mainland Portugal from 00:00 to 23:59 on Wednesday 11 November: schools and non-essential public services closed, outdoor events suspended, no access to parks and wooded areas at risk of falling trees, nor to the shoreline, rivers and flood-prone areas. At ANEPC's briefing on Wednesday (published 16:49 UTC) the Minister of Internal Administration said it would not be kept on Thursday, though repairs might be needed and some services might stay limited. (Read by PrompTugal.news 11 Nov 18:37 UTC; [source](https://portugal.gov.pt/gc25/comunicacao/noticias/governo-declara-situacao-de-alerta-em-todo-o-territorio-continental-1).)
- **The Minister of Internal Administration** asked people to stay at home where possible and avoid unnecessary travel: an appeal, not an order. (Read 10 Nov 17:01 UTC; [source](https://portugal.gov.pt/gc25/comunicacao/noticias/mau-tempo-governo-declara-situacao-alerta-).)
- **ANEPC** (civil protection) raised its special readiness state and sent a public SMS warning about Wednesday. Its 12:00 situation update counted 538 incidents, none significant; 2,222 operatives and 688 vehicles deployed; 23 municipal and 2 district emergency plans active. (Read 11 Nov 18:37 UTC; [source](https://prociv.gov.pt/pt/noticias/).)
- **The Maritime Authority and the Navy** forecast a considerable worsening of the weather and the sea from 06:00 on Wednesday to midday on Thursday. This is their forecast for the sea and the coast, not IPMA's land warning. (Read 10 Nov 16:58 UTC, not re-checked by PrompTugal.news; [source](https://www.amn.pt/Media/Paginas/DetalheNoticia.aspx?nid=7101).)

## What the IPMA data on this site can and cannot show

- **It shows now, not then.** The other pages of this site read each source's `latest.json` from data.promptugal.news: IPMA's files as they were at the last hourly read. They do not hold the storm's files.
- **The history is of runs, not values.** Each source's `reads.json` logs the collector's runs: the address read, the HTTP status, the size, the start of a checksum and whether the bytes changed. It does not keep the warnings or readings themselves, so it cannot replay the storm.
- **The collectors started after the storm.** The IPMA warnings card gives its start as 17 November 2026 (https://data.promptugal.news/api/v1/ipma-warnings/card.json, read 18 November at 10:55 UTC), five days after Storm Charles. The dated copies in its vault begin then too.
- **The storm's own files are kept elsewhere.** data.promptugal.news lists a Storm Charles archive: the data files behind storm-charles.promptugal.news, for Lisbon and Madeira, 10 to 12 November 2026 (https://data.promptugal.news/sources/storm-charles-archive/index.md, listed in https://data.promptugal.news/llms.txt, read 18 November at 10:55 UTC). This site does not draw them yet.
